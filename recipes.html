    Classic Spaghetti Carbonara

    Ingredients

    • 350g Spaghetti or Rigatoni
    • 4 fresh Egg yolks + 1 whole Egg
    • 100g Pecorino Romano cheese (finely grated)
    • 150g Guanciale (cured pork cheek)
    • Freshly cracked black pepper

    Method

    Cook spaghetti in salted boiling water until al dente. In a pan, crisp the guanciale over medium heat until golden.

    Whisk egg yolks with grated Pecorino and black pepper. Toss hot pasta with guanciale fat, remove from heat, and quickly stir in the egg mixture with a splash of pasta water until creamy.

    Neapolitan Margherita Pizza

    Ingredients

    • 250g Fermented Pizza Dough
    • 100g San Marzano crushed tomatoes
    • 120g Fresh Fior di Latte Mozzarella
    • Fresh Basil leaves
    • Extra Virgin Olive Oil & Salt

    Method

    Stretch dough ball gently by hand onto a baking tray or pizza stone. Spread crushed tomatoes evenly.

    Add torn mozzarella pieces, and season with salt and olive oil. Bake at maximum oven temperature (250°C+) until crust rises with charred leopard spots. Top with fresh basil before serving.

    PHILOSOPHY

    Italian Cooking Golden Rules

    1. Organic Ingredients

    Italian cuisine relies on 3–4 peak-season ingredients. Always choose extra virgin olive oil, fresh herbs, and real DOP cheeses.

    2. Simplicity Over Heavy Sauces

    No heavy cream in Carbonara or ketchup on pizza. Let the natural flavors of ripe tomatoes, garlic, and basil shine.

    3. Respecting Heritage

    Traditional Italian recipes represent regional identity. Preserve classical techniques while cooking with passion.

    FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

    Culinary Secrets & Tips

    We recommend serving cooked pasta immediately to enjoy the perfect al dente texture. Pizza dough can be cold-fermented in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 48 hours to enhance flavor development.

    While authentic Roman Carbonara requires Guanciale and Pecorino Romano, you can substitute Guanciale with high-quality Pancetta or unsmoked bacon, and Pecorino with aged Parmigiano-Reggiano for a milder taste.

    The secret lies in emulsifying the starchy hot pasta water with the egg yolks, grated cheese, and rendered fat from the Guanciale off the heat. Mixing off the direct heat prevents the eggs from scrambling.
